## Purpose
Test whether the product can sustain itself. A clear value proposition still fails if the money model does not work.

## Structure
- **Value metric**: the unit the customer pays for (seat, usage, outcome) and why it tracks value received.
- **Pricing model options**: 2-3 candidates (subscription, usage, freemium, one-time) with the trade-off of each.
- **Unit economics assumptions**: rough cost to serve one customer, expected price, gross margin direction. Mark each number as [assumption] unless given.
- **Acquisition assumption**: how a customer is reached and the rough cost direction.
- **Risks**: where the model breaks (low willingness to pay, high serve cost, long payback).

## Method
1. Start from willingness to pay and the value metric, not from a price.
2. State the riskiest economic assumption and the cheapest way to test it (a pricing page test, 5 sales conversations).
3. Do not fabricate market sizes or benchmark margins; if cited, label them as needing verification.

## Anti-patterns
- Picking a price with no value metric behind it.
- Freemium chosen by default with no plan for conversion.
- Treating made-up CAC/LTV numbers as facts.

## When NOT to use
For internal tools or pre-revenue throwaway probes, defer pricing and note it as out of scope for now.
