## Purpose
Understand the field the product enters: not just named competitors, but the substitutes and workarounds users already trust. The wedge is usually found in switching costs, not feature lists.

## Structure
- **Direct**: products solving the same job the same way. For each: who it serves, its core strength, its weakness you can exploit.
- **Indirect**: products solving the same job a different way.
- **Substitutes / status quo**: spreadsheets, manual process, "do nothing". Often the real competitor.
- **Switching analysis**: what would have to be true for a user to leave their current solution (cost, habit, data lock-in, trust).
- **Gap**: the underserved job or segment, stated as a sentence, not a feature.

## Method
1. Start from the user's current behavior, not from a list of brands.
2. For each rival, separate marketing claims from observable behavior; do not restate a competitor's site copy as fact.
3. End with one honest answer to: why would a user switch, and why now.

## Anti-patterns
- Feature-comparison tables that ignore why users actually stay.
- Inventing competitor metrics, pricing, or market share. If unknown, say so and mark what to verify.
- Declaring "no competitors": the status quo always competes.

## When NOT to use
For an internal tool with a captive audience, deep competitive work is low value; note the status quo and move on.
