## Purpose
Give the team one concrete human to build for. A good persona constrains decisions; a generic one ("busy professional") constrains nothing.

## Structure
- **Name and one-line identity** (role + context, not a stock photo).
- **Top goals** (what success looks like for them).
- **Context**: tools, constraints, environment, level of expertise.
- **Pains and workarounds**: what hurts today and what they do instead.
- **Triggers**: the moment they would seek a solution.
- **Evidence vs assumption**: tag each attribute as observed (interview, data) or [assumption].
- **Anti-persona**: who this product deliberately does not serve.

## Method
1. Build from real signals where they exist; where they do not, mark assumptions and list what to validate.
2. Prefer one primary persona over three weak ones.
3. Make at least one attribute decision-relevant (something that would change a design or scope call).

## Anti-patterns
- Demographic trivia that never affects a decision.
- Three personas that are really one.
- Fabricated quotes or stats presented as research.

## When NOT to use
For a single-stakeholder internal tool, a one-line user description is enough.
